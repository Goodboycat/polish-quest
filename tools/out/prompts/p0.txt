THE 19 RULES (every prompt serves one or more of these)
1. Tutorial: No text tutorials. A hand or pointer goes to each part of the game one by one and shows what it is, only when the player is curious. Never a wall of steps.
2. Notifications: Nothing blocks the screen. Messages, rewards and warnings go into a small collapsible log in a corner. The center always stays clear.
3. Smooth UI: Popups, buttons, counters and transitions all use the same timing and easing. Nothing snaps or pops in. Only move and fade things so it stays smooth on weak phones.
4. One-tap controls: Every button works on one tap, even pause, restart and back to title. Tapping outside a popup closes it. Moves can happen at the same time, like dashing while moving and attacking.
5. Earned abilities: Hidden until the player earns them. No locked buttons. A short pointer moment when one unlocks.
6. Learn by playing: No reading. If a system can be beaten without understanding it, redesign it so understanding it is what wins.
7. Combat feel: Hits have weight, with knockback, a short hit-pause and stretchy attacks. Enemies are a real threat.
8. Variety: Every run or season feels different, with new discoveries, new enemy behavior, a crisis or event, and steady progress.
9. Economy: Money buys decisions, not more of the same. Even a rich player still has strategy.
10. Retention: Find where players quit and give them a reason to continue right there.
11. Performance: Batch drawing, reuse objects, create nothing every frame, cache everything. Stable on the weakest phone before any new feature.
12. Structure and safety: Start every system before anything uses it, keep things modular, use versioned saves, fix bugs one by one, save a checkpoint after every task.
13. Purpose and goal: A purpose you can say in one sentence, a main goal with milestones, a small progress tracker, everything serves the goal, and a real ending.
14. Short goals: Always one or two goals that take a few minutes. When one finishes, the next appears.
15. Story cinematic: At the start, at key turning points and at the ending. Characters with different voices, one-tap skip, remembers what was seen. Built inside the game, no video files.
16. Dialogue: Short, in character, about what just happened, reacts to choices. A bottom box that never covers the action, with fade-ins.
17. VFX with purpose: Wind-up, main effect, impact, lingering trail. Each layer tells the player something. No effect just for looks. Reused particles and capped overdraw.
18. Finisher: Only for big moments. Build-up, a slow-motion beat, a camera push, heavier effects, tied to the story. Skippable. Never on normal kills.
19. Emotional payoff: Plan the loss, sacrifice, reunion and victory on purpose. Set each one up first, then use the cinematic, the finisher, a music shift and a quiet moment.
